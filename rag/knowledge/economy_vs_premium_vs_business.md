# Economy vs Premium Economy vs Business

Airline cabins are usually split into Economy, Premium Economy, and Business
(First is rarer and more expensive). Understanding the differences helps you
choose the right product for a trip without overpaying.

**Economy** is the standard cabin. Seats are densest, recline is limited, and
meals (when included) are basic. On long-haul flights you typically get a
seatback screen or shared entertainment, one free checked bag on some
carriers, and the lowest fare. Economy is fine for short trips or when budget
is the priority. On overnight intercontinental flights, sleep quality can be
poor because seats do not fully recline and neighbors are close.

**Premium Economy** sits between Economy and Business. You usually get a wider
seat, more pitch (legroom), a larger screen, better meals, and sometimes
priority boarding. It is popular for long-haul night flights when you want
more comfort without paying a full Business fare. Rules vary by airline: some
allow extra baggage; others only improve the seat and service.

**Business** is designed for rest and work. Long-haul Business often means a
lie-flat or angled-flat seat, lounge access, premium dining, and generous
baggage. It costs substantially more than Economy. For daytime short-haul
flights, “Business” may simply mean a blocked middle seat and better catering
rather than a full suite.

When comparing fares, check what is included (bag, seat selection, changes)
and the total trip length. A cheap Economy ticket with paid bags and seats can
approach Premium Economy. For flights over eight hours, many travelers find
Premium Economy the best value compromise.
