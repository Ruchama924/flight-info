# What Is a Layover and Connection Risk

A **layover** (also called a connection) is the time you spend at an airport
between two flights on the same itinerary. Instead of one nonstop flight, you
change planes in a hub city and continue to your final destination.

**Minimum connection time (MCT)** is the shortest interval an airport and
airline consider workable for a legal connection. Domestic connections may be
45–60 minutes; international connections, especially when you must clear
immigration or security again, often need 90–180 minutes. Booking engines
usually refuse connections below MCT, but “legal” does not mean comfortable.

**Connection risk** rises when:
- The layover is barely above MCT
- Your inbound flight is often delayed
- You must change terminals or airports
- You fly into a busy hub late in the day (fewer recovery options)
- You carry bags that must be re-checked (more common on separate tickets)

If you miss a connection on a **single ticket**, the airline normally rebooks
you on a later flight at no fare difference. On **separate tickets**, you are
usually treated as a no-show on the next flight and must buy a new ticket.

Tips: prefer longer layovers for international trips; avoid the last flight of
the day when possible; download the airline app for gate changes; and know
where your bags are tagged (final destination vs local). A 2–3 hour layover is
often safer than a 50-minute “tight” connection, even if the shorter option
looks cheaper.
