# Baggage Allowance Basics

Baggage rules are set by the airline and the fare brand you bought, not only
by cabin class. Always check the ticket’s baggage section before packing.

**Carry-on (cabin bag)** is what you bring into the cabin. Most airlines allow
one cabin bag plus a small personal item (purse, laptop bag). Size and weight
limits differ: low-cost carriers are stricter and often charge for a larger
cabin bag. Liquids in cabin bags must follow security rules (typically
containers of 100 ml / 3.4 oz or less in a clear bag).

**Checked baggage** goes in the hold. Traditional full-service airlines often
include one free checked bag on long-haul Economy tickets; basic/“light” fares
may include none. Business and Premium Economy usually include more weight or
an extra piece. Weight caps of 23 kg (50 lb) per bag are common; some routes
use piece-based rules instead of a single weight pool.

**Excess baggage** fees apply if you exceed piece count, weight, or size.
Sports equipment and musical instruments may need special booking. Connecting
on one ticket usually means bags are checked through to the final city;
separate tickets often require collecting and re-checking bags.

Practical advice: weigh bags at home, leave a little margin under the limit,
put essentials and a change of clothes in the cabin bag in case a checked bag
is delayed, and photograph valuables. If traveling with a codeshare itinerary,
follow the **marketing airline’s** published baggage rules unless the ticket
says otherwise—or confirm with both carriers when unsure.
