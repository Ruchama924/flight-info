# What Is a Codeshare Flight

A **codeshare** is when one flight is sold under two or more airline codes.
The **operating airline** flies the aircraft and employs the crew. One or more
**marketing airlines** sell seats on that same flight using their own flight
numbers.

Example: you buy ticket `BA4567`, but the aircraft is operated by another
carrier as `AA123`. At the airport and on the departure board you may see the
operating flight number; your boarding pass can show either code. In our
FlightAdvisor details view (Slice 3), codeshares appear as
**“Operated by: {airline_name} ({flight_iata})”** when AviationStack provides
that data—so the marketing brand on your ticket may differ from who actually
flies the plane.

Why airlines do this: alliances and partnerships fill networks without flying
every route themselves. You can earn/redeem miles on a partner and get a
single itinerary across brands.

What to watch for:
- **Aircraft and product** follow the operating airline (seat map, Wi‑Fi, meals)
- **Airport experience** (check-in desks, lounges) may follow marketing or
  operating rules depending on the airport
- **Baggage allowance** is usually governed by the ticketed (marketing)
  carrier’s rules on through tickets—confirm if unsure
- Irregular operations are handled by the operating airline’s ground staff

When reading search results, treat the displayed airline as the marketing
brand and open flight details to see if a codeshare operator is listed. That
continuity helps you know whose plane you will board and whose onboard product
to expect.
