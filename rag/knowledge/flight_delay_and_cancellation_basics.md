# Flight Delay and Cancellation Basics

Delays and cancellations happen for weather, air-traffic control, crew limits,
maintenance, and airport congestion. Knowing your rights and practical next
steps reduces stress when plans change.

**Delay** means the flight still operates but leaves (or arrives) later than
scheduled. Short delays may only change the gate time; longer delays can cause
missed connections. Airlines update estimated times in their apps and on airport
boards—refresh often.

**Cancellation** means the flight will not operate. On a single ticket the
airline must typically rebook you on an alternative flight, and in some regions
(for example under EU261-style rules) may owe care (meals, hotel) or
compensation depending on the cause and length of disruption. Rules vary by
country and whether the cause was “extraordinary” (severe weather, ATC strikes)
versus within the airline’s control.

What you should do:
1. Check the airline app before going to the airport when storms are forecast
2. If connecting, ask about protected rebooking as soon as a delay appears
3. Keep booking reference, passport, and toiletries in your cabin bag
4. Ask whether overnight hotels or meal vouchers apply
5. Photograph boarding passes and delay notices if you later claim expenses

**Separate tickets** give weaker protection: a delay on flight A does not
obligate airline B to wait or refund. Travel insurance or flexible fares help.

Not every delay is compensable, but clear documentation and early contact with
the airline improve outcomes. For connections, a longer layover is the simplest
way to absorb moderate delays without missing the next flight.
